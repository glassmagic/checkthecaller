How your details reach a scammer

    You sign up to something

    Long terms and conditions let your details be shared and sold, quite legally.

  2. 2
    A company is hacked

    Stolen details are sold on to criminals.

  3. 3
    You confirm details on a cold call

    Saying “yes, that’s me” turns a guess into a fact they can sell. This is the one you control.

PART ONE · YOUR DIGITAL FOOTPRINT

Your photos carry hidden details

  • When it was taken

    Date and time, to the second

  • Exactly where you were

    Often to within a few metres

  • Which way you were facing

    And whether you were moving

  • …and dozens more, depending on your phone’s settings. Anyone you send the photo to may be able to read them.
One thing to do this week

Turn off “location” in your camera settings. Not sure how? Ask a family member or your phone shop.

PART TWO · WHEN THE PHONE RINGS

It depends who’s calling

The phone rings

You know them

The number is saved in your phone, or the voice is immediately familiar.

Chat as normal. If the talk turns to money or codes, slow right down.

You don’t

An unknown voice, a silent line, or a withheld number.

Use the three responses on the next page.

PART TWO · WHEN THE PHONE RINGS

Three responses to learn by heart

1
Silence with silence

A silent call gets nothing from you. Say nothing and hang up.

2
Question with question

“Who’s calling?” “Which company?” “Why do you need that?”

3
Yes with no

Never agree in haste. “No. I’ll call you back on the official number.”

Practise them until they come out automatically, like braking in a car.

PART TWO · WHEN THE PHONE PINGS

A message arrives: four steps before you tap

  1. Stop

    Don’t tap the link or reply.

  2. Check

    Use the real app, or the number on your bank card.

  3. Report

    Forward the text to 7726. It’s free.

  4. Ask

    Get a second opinion from someone you trust.

Same four steps for:
  • Texts
  • WhatsApp
  • Facebook
  • Email

PART TWO · WHEN A CODE ASKS TO BE SCANNED

QR codes: if in doubt, don’t scan

A QR code is a web link you can’t read.

Anyone can make one and stick it anywhere.

  • Never pay via a QR code
  • Never download an app via a QR code
  • Never scan a sticker on a wall, meter or menu

PART TWO · A PRACTICAL STEP

Blocking a number takes three taps

Android phones
  1. Open the message from the sender.
  2. Tap the three dots, top right.
  3. Tap Block.
iPhones
  1. Open the message from the sender.
  2. Tap the name or number at the top.
  3. Tap Info, then Block this Caller.

Not sure? Ask a family member or your phone shop. They will happily show you.

Warning sign 1 of 3

A call you were not expecting

Scammers can copy your bank’s name and number. That does not prove who is calling.

Question with question: “Who’s calling? Which company? Why do you need that?”

Warning sign 2 of 3

Being rushed or frightened

Pressure to act quickly makes it harder to stop and check whether the caller is telling the truth.

Yes with no: never agree in haste. You can end the call.

Warning sign 3 of 3

Move money to “safety”

Your bank will never ask you to move money to a “safe account”. It could belong to the scammer.

Hang up and contact your bank yourself, on the number on your card.

Remember these four steps

Stop, check, report, ask

  1. Stop. Hang up. Don’t follow the caller’s instructions.
  2. Check. Wait five minutes, then call the number on your bank card.
  3. Report. Tell your bank. Forward scam texts to 7726, free.
  4. Ask. Get a second opinion from someone you trust.
